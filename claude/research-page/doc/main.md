## Microbrands 2023–2026

A few hundred brands, most selling direct from their own site in runs of 50 to 500, and the dial is what they are talked about for. The brands that rose in these three years did it with a dial material or a dial process: stone (Dennison, Baltic Prismic Stone, Toledano & Chan), enamel under $3,000 (Selten, anOrdain, Erebus), guilloché cut by machine or by hand (Anoma, Atelier Wen, Zelos), stamped or lasered textures (Christopher Ward), or a colour story (Studio Underd0g). No credible census of brand numbers exists; the fairs give the only counts, and they are growing: Windup NYC from 140 brands in 2025 to about 170 in 2026, Geneva Watch Days from 17 brands in 2020 to 71 in 2026 ([Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/podcasts/previewing-the-2026-windup-watch-fair-nyc/) \[S\], [WorldTempus](https://en.worldtempus.com/article/business-news/geneva-watch-days-2026-seventh-edition-unites-entire-geneva-watchmaking-ecosystem-82960.html) \[P\]). Fratello already calls 71 "too many" ([Fratello](https://www.fratellowatches.com/the-geneva-watch-days-2026-delivered/) \[P\]).

## Dial stories

Eight accounts of a dial getting made. The pattern across them: the dial that made a brand was an accident, a tool built from scratch, or one artisan found after a long search, and the cost was always in rejects and hours, not in drawing. Each fact carries its source; where a detail is only inferred it says so.

**anOrdain's fumé was a warped reject.** anOrdain enamelled on copper, but copper made translucent colours too dark, so in 2017 they tried silver blanks. Silver domed in the kiln; when the enamel was ground flat it sat thicker and darker at the edge, thinner in the centre. That was the gradient ([anOrdain](https://anordain.com/blogs/news/the-happy-accident-of-the-fume-dial) \[P\]). A domed dial will not sit on a movement, so the Struthers pointed them to a Birmingham Jewellery Quarter toolmaker who die-stamps the blanks on century-old medal presses ([anOrdain](https://anordain.com/blogs/news/the-world-s-first-fume-enamel-dial) \[P\]). About seven layers, about 1 mm of enamel, a couple of rejects in each batch of six \[P\]; Monochrome saw a 30 % reject rate overall and 14 hours of kiln time a batch ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/just-because-learning-the-art-of-vitreous-enamel-dialmaking-thanks-to-anordain/) \[R\]). Model 1 fumé was about $2,340 in 2020 ([W&W](https://wornandwound.com/anordain-brings-their-stunning-fume-dials-to-the-model-1-in-four-variants) \[R\]).

**Christopher Ward picked a laser over a rose engine.** The 2024 Bel Canto Classic swapped the sunray plate for a guilloché-look wave that is laser-etched; the sharp points give it away ([T+T](https://timeandtidewatches.com/christopher-ward-c1-bel-canto-classic-introducing/) \[R\]). The arithmetic: about 2 hours a dial by laser against at least 6 by hand, one machine giving about 12 dials a day with near-total QC pass, where hand work gave perhaps 14 a week before rejects. Monthly output went from 50 in Nov 2022 to 500 in Sept 2024 ([WatchGecko](https://watchgecko.com/blogs/magazine/christopher-ward-c1-bel-canto-classic-review) \[R\]). Finishing partner APJ in Biel hired three people for the hand-finished surfaces \[R\]; SJX confirms the "laser-etched guilloché" wording ([SJX](https://watchesbysjx.com/2024/11/christopher-ward-c1-bel-canto-classic.html) \[R\]). No source supports an in-house CW dial shop; the dials come from partners \[I\].

**Atelier Wen's guilloché comes from a cave.** Cheng Yucai quit his job after seeing an engraved enamel tobacco box, spent years building his own rose engine because working ones are rare and usually damaged, and set up in a mountain cave near Xinmi. Each copper blank is sanded and burned flat to 0.1 mm, mounted and cut with a pumping motion; a dial takes up to 30 hours and "any tiny imperfection is enough to scrap the entire dial" ([T+T, 2022](https://timeandtidewatches.com/?p=77256) \[R\]). Launch price $2,088; the Perception is $3,288 today, against Garrick at £6,500 and Laine at CHF 11,500 for hand guilloché ([T+T](https://timeandtidewatches.com/best-guilloche-dial-watches/) \[P\]).

**Selten found one enamellist.** Leonardo Tsai (Selten, Hong Kong, 2017) met Qin, a self-taught enameller, engraver and jeweller, in 2021 while hunting for a dial partner ([T+T](https://timeandtidewatches.com/selten-metiers-dart-grand-feu-enamel-series-micro-mondays/) \[R\]). Each blank is hand-carved with about 700 grooves, up to five hours before any colour, then coated in translucent enamel and printed ([Monochrome](https://monochrome-watches.com/review-vibrant-stylish-and-accessible-selten-grand-feu-enamel-collection-kickstarter-price) \[R\]); enamelling adds 4–5 hours a dial \[R\]. Pre-order $1,899–2,199, rising to $2,599–2,899, deliveries from April 2025 \[R\]. Firing counts and reject rates are not given anywhere \[I: gap\].

**Christopher Ward built a stamping tool for a boiled sweet.** The Twelve 36 "Pick 'n' Mix" took over two months to develop a stamped texture meant to read as crystallised sugar; no off-the-shelf tool gave the effect, so one was made. Each dial fades from light at 12 to saturated at 6 like dye pooling at the base of a sweet; six colours, 50 each, £1,695 ([Barrington, retailer blog](https://barringtonwatchwinders.com/blogs/blog/the-christopher-ward-twelve-pick-n-mix-is-proof-that-watches-dont-always-have-to-take-themselves-seriously) \[B\]). Worn & Wound confirms the brief, the colours and the $2,350 price but not the tooling ([W&W, Jul 2026](https://wornandwound.com/a-watch-worthy-of-a-dentist-visit-the-christopher-ward-twelve-36-ti-pick-n-mix-limited-edition/) \[R\]).

**Studio Underd0g's Watermel0n doubled in price on the same dial.** The 2021 Kickstarter run at $450 already had the recipe: a textured centre darkening to the edge, a smooth tachymeter ring, a three-tone 30-minute counter, seed indices, C3 ([W&W](https://wornandwound.com/review/review-the-colorful-studio-underd0g-chronographs/) \[R\]). Gen 2 at $800 describes a "coarse dégradé top dial" over a pink base, assembly in Britain and full independent QC by Horologium ([T+T shop](https://shop.timeandtidewatches.com/products/studio-underd0g-watermel0n-gen-2) \[S\]). The added value is where it is assembled and who inspects it, not the dial technique \[D\]. No opened source names the dial supplier.

**Straum carried a volcano home.** Co-founders Lasse and Øystein sailed to Jan Mayen; the core dials' ridged textures and Moss Green and Glacier White came from the island ([W&W](https://wornandwound.com/out-of-office-12-days-and-3500-miles-through-scandinavia-with-the-straum-jan-mayen/) \[R\]). For the Basalt edition the rock itself is cut into thin slices and its cavities left open over a luminous base; developed with people in Norway, Scotland, Switzerland and China ([T+T shop](https://shop.timeandtidewatches.com/collections/straum-jan-mayen-basalt) \[S\]). The brand's own journal was unreachable \[I\].

**Toledano & Chan's lapis gave way to mother-of-pearl.** The B/1 launched 9 May 2024 at $4,000, 175 numbered pieces; its lapis dial carries no markers or branding so the stone's flaws are the design ([W&W](https://wornandwound.com/?p=129392) \[R\]). By Jan 2025 the B/1.2 had Tahitian mother-of-pearl at about £4,400, up from £3,229, and no reason given ([WatchGecko](https://www.watchgecko.com/blogs/magazine/hands-on-review-with-the-new-toledano-chan-b12) \[R\]). Stone supply and breakage is the plausible driver; nothing opened confirms it \[I\].

**Lebois & Co's Pantone died on the enamel** is told under Their pain. The Kickstarter-era stories (Chronotechna's grey-patched dials, Soeroadster's two greens, Tektora's rejected first sample) wait on pages this session cannot open; they are listed there as leads.

## How they design and produce dials

The founder draws, a 3D model and a render carry the intent to the factory, and a physical sample decides. No opened founder interview names Illustrator or Figma; the named chain is SolidWorks or Rhino, then KeyShot, and the render is used as the brief because "it leaves no room for error" ([KeyShot on Wilbur](https://www.keyshot.com/customers/wilbur-used-keyshot-to-communicate-watch-design-intent-to-factories/) \[P\]). The Swiss trade sells dials at about CHF 300 average and mostly refuses orders under 100; the Chinese trade's minimums fell from 1,000 to 300 over a decade; one Swiss house takes single pieces. Costs are stated only in forum posts: a dial-and-hands sample round from about $500, and 9 to 15 months from first drawing to delivery \[I\].

The pattern in one story: Emmanuel Gueit drew a perforated dial for Ikepod, the owner rejected it on paper, samples were made anyway, and the samples won ([Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/a486355/interview-emmanuel-gueit/) \[R\]). Paper does not decide; the sample does. That is why the colour and lume surprises in Their pain all land at sample one.

## Their pain

Lume is what goes wrong most, then colour drifting between the approved sample and the batch, then alignment. Renders oversell exactly the two things a dial tool draws worst: texture and glow. Of 25 incidents found (2017–2026), 10 are lume, 4 colour, 4 alignment, 4 render-against-reality, 3 enamel reject rates, 2 print legibility \[D\]. The richest first-person accounts sit in Kickstarter and BackerKit updates, which this session's network cannot open; those are listed as leads at the end, unverified.

A story that holds the pattern: Lebois & Co specified the hands of its P25 in a dark-blue Pantone at the design stage. When the first enamel dials came back from Donzé Cadrans, the Pantone no longer matched the fired enamel, and a lighter blue had to be picked against the real dial, with more dial samples ordered to confirm it ([WatchProSite, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.watchprosite.com/digest/lebois-co-p25-collection-update/) \[P\]). The colour was right on screen and wrong on the bench, and the only fix was a physical sample. Nodus had the same lesson from the other side: field testers sent the Nomad GMT prototypes back for thicker, brighter lume and bolder cardinal indices, and the dial was redrawn whole ([Gear Patrol, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.gearpatrol.com/watches/nodus-nomad/) \[R\]).

## What already serves them

Nothing serves the job Dial Tool does. Configurators are swap menus built in-house; render studios and freelancers sell pictures, not production files; the only dedicated "dial" software is a SOLIDWORKS add-in for industrial users. Brands and hobbyists still draw dials in Illustrator or Inkscape and fight real-size preview, file size and placement by hand. The one consumer attempt at a web dial editor, Oblako in 2013, failed to fund ([Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/oblako-watches-stratus-project/) \[B\]). What has changed since: UV-print bureaus now print a single dial from any file for about $25, so the old minimum-order wall is gone for a one-off maker \[I, bureau sites not opened\].

Undone is the configurator everyone cites, and it shows the ceiling of the genre: some two dozen preset dials, one line of custom text, hands in three styles and nine colours, a visualiser the team built itself, and a tool taken offline whenever demand outran the workshop ([Worn & Wound](https://wornandwound.com/design-your-own-undone-watch/) \[R\], [aBlogtoWatch](https://ablogtowatch.com/customizable-undone-urban-chronograph-watches) \[R\]). Ariel Adams' verdict on the whole category: "no watchmaker has yet to keep the lights on with the income from configured watches" ([aBlogtoWatch, 2023](https://www.ablogtowatch.com/ariels-thoughts-making-sense-of-wrist-watch-customization/) \[P\]).

## Where Dial Tool fits

The market is small, open and not lucrative: a few hundred brands, each drawing one to three dials a year, served today by Illustrator, a CAD package, KeyShot and the factory's own draughtsman. Nothing in that chain shows a dial at true size with its lume, its finest printable line and its parts placed against feet and apertures, and that gap is exactly where the sample-one surprises in Their pain happen. Dial Tool already does the true-size vector part (brief §3: PDF and SVG at size in mm, production masks, print and spec sheets). What it would need for a microbrand is the proof layer: lume as an ink with a grade and a night view, the finest-line check per method, a spec sheet a factory can act on. What would not sell is a consumer configurator or a renderer. Say it plainly: as a product this is a niche of hundreds, best treated as Okay's own instrument and a portfolio piece, with a paid version only if a bureau or a mod shop wants to bundle it \[D\].

## Lume

Lume has two colours and a clock: its daylight colour, its glow colour, and how long it stays above the eye's limit, \~0.4 mcd/m² after a 20-minute charge at 400 lux. v1 said no absolute luminance existed; it does.

## Lume preview

One Day · Night switch in show mode: by night the whole watch goes through one colour matrix, the lume is drawn on top in its glow colour, and a "minutes since charged" slider sets how bright. It is new work (no colour matrix exists in the code), cheap to draw, and waits on one decision: is Lume a property of an ink or of a part?

### The recipe, against the code

### Adversarial pass

1. A screen can't glow brighter than its white. "Brighter" means more contrast against a darker night, so the night's darkness is the real control, not the lume's.
2. Buyers' picture of lume comes from long exposures (Fratello, Bert's lume shots). Minute 0 with bloom is that photo look; 20–45 minutes is the eye's. One slider covers both, but the default must be the eye's or the tool flatters.
3. Exact-colour inks merge a lume white with a printed white of the same hex. Either lume is a part's property, or the A | B split carries it. Both touch #545's locked shape: Okay's call.
4. The model's absolute end point (0.4 mcd/m²) is sourced; its curve between 0 and 60 min rests on one Nemoto chart quoted in a forum post. A second curve (Kronometric's 10 → 60 min, 120 → 19 mcd/m²) agrees within about a third \[D\]. Good enough for a look, not for a spec.
5. The Intel Mac is untested for every blur (#506 verify). Nothing here should ship before that answer.

### What shows at show mode's size

A 40 mm watch drawn 800 px wide is 50 µm a pixel \[D\]. An 8 µm layer and a 15 µm edge wobble are below a pixel. In order of what shows: height (built, #545); closing-up of thin strokes and counters (a spread in µm per ink); misregistration (a shift in µm, seen only where two inks meet); edge grain (a loupe view only). The edge recipe, feTurbulence with feDisplacementMap ([Codrops, Soueidan 2019](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=37751)), is written for textures in general \[I\]; its Safari bug dates from 2019.

### The order of layers

Every source agrees on one stack, which is show mode's drawing order.

1. Blank: brass 0.4–0.7 mm, cut up to 400 t at 750 °C ([TAG](https://magazine.tagheuer.com/en/2024/12/16/the-art-of-tag-heuer-dial-finishes/) \[P\]).
2. Base finish on bare metal: sunray, grain, blast ([Horopedia](https://horopedia.org/lacquered-dials/) · [Glashütte](https://www.glashuette-original.com/en/the-brand-glashutte-original/our-expertise-in-dials/) \[P\]). "Never over print" is inferred from the order, not stated \[I\].
3. Colour: galvanic 0.01–15 µm, PVD or lacquer; clear varnish against oxidation (Patek visit \[P\]).
4. Print: one colour a pass, under 20 µm register, UV or oven cure ([Glashütte](https://www.timeandwatches.com/2025/06/the-face-of-time-dial-manufacture-at.html) \[P\]); on enamel, a 24 µm ceramic cliché, resin inks at up to 140 °C ([anOrdain](https://anordain.com/pages/dial-printing) \[P\]).
5. Optional clear coat, oven \~80 °C ([Horopedia](https://horopedia.org/?p=35645) \[P\]).
6. Applied indices, friction-fit pins (Fehr visit \[F\]).
7. Lume last: by hand, pad print for fine deposits (glass or ceramic cliché, ceramic blade), screen for big bright spots ([RC Tritec](https://www.rctritec.com/en/superluminova/varnish-and-dilutor) \[P\]).

A lateral route that suits Okay's kit, if he has a diode laser: spray paint on brass, burn the design out in LightBurn, then etch in ferric; fine letters went at 10 minutes in one test ([diyAudio](https://www.diyaudio.com/community/threads/howto-etching-a-brass-frontplate.371221/) \[F\]).

## Rendering and presentation

Stay in SVG: paths and gradients carry every sun-driven finish within \~7 ms of a flat plate; only grain and enamel need filters, cached as a picture. The press distrusts renders that show one light; a dial shown under several suns and at night is the honest picture.

### Why one light lies

| Finding | Source |
| --- | --- |
| A render captures one lighting scenario; on screen a sunburst or guilloché reads as a light-to-dark gradient, in the metal it changes with the angle and the room | [Fratello, Henry Black, 5 Jul 2026](https://www.fratellowatches.com/why-watch-renders-so-frequently-leave-us-underwhelmed/) \[R\], read whole in the Mac's browser |
| Oris Artelier Hölstein Edition 2026: flat in launch CGI; its polished sub-dial, brushed and polished contrast and domed dial only showed in the metal | same \[R\] |
| A brand promised a sunburst in renders and delivered it matte; polished reflections render as grey blobs | [Time+Tide, Fergus Nash, 7 Feb 2023](https://timeandtidewatches.com/what-tweaks-my-tourb-digital-render/) \[R\] |
| Tone shifts with the light's colour; polished shows its surroundings, brushed shows broad light and dark bands; judge a watch from several photos in different light | [Time+Tide, Buffy Acacia, 30 Mar 2024](https://timeandtidewatches.com/how-watches-can-change-in-photos-versus-reality/) \[R\] |

So the honest export is a light-sweep sheet: one dial, the sun at three or four angles, then the night. It is #17's variation grid with the sun as the variable, and nothing new to export (show mode's export is already a picture).

### The night, as the research describes it

| Part | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blue shift | rod luminance pulls colour toward a blue \~0.03, 0.03 below the white point (chromaticity) | [Thompson, Shirley, Ferwerda 2002, JGT 7(1)](https://jamesferwerda.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/j09_thompson02_jgt.pdf) \[P\] |
| Loss of detail | Gaussian blur, then edges sharpened back (band-pass raised to 1/1.25) | same \[P\] |
| Grain | Gaussian noise, σ 0.0125 | same \[P\] |
| Night colour | luminance × (1.05, 0.97, 1.27), blended by the rod share 0.04 / (0.04 + Y) | [Krawczyk, Myszkowski, Seidel 2005](https://resources.mpi-inf.mpg.de/hdr/peffects/krawczyk05sccg.pdf) \[P\] |
| Film's version | shoot \~2 stops under, unlit areas blue, glare round light sources; moonlight only looks blue (Purkinje effect) | [Day for night, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_for_night) \[I\] |

As one feColorMatrix \[D, from Krawczyk's constants with Rec. 709 weights\]: R row 0.2232 · 0.7510 · 0.0758, G 0.2062 · 0.6937 · 0.0700, B 0.2700 · 0.9083 · 0.0917, each row × \~0.25 for two stops. Keeping the lume sharp while the plate softens is Claude's choice; the paper blurs everything and sharpens the edges back. Noise is a turbulence filter, the costly kind: under the shenanigans switch if at all.

### Recipes and their catches

| Catch | Fix | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Touching wedges leave a light seam: each shape is anti-aliased alone | merge every wedge of one tone into one path: 4–8 paths, not 240 | [skia-discuss](https://groups.google.com/d/msg/skia-discuss/NlKpD2X_5uc/Vuwd-kyYBwAJ) \[P\]; [WebKit 35211](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=35211), open since 2010, touched Apr 2026 \[P\] |
| Browsers disagree on filter colour space (default linearRGB) | set color-interpolation-filters="sRGB" on every filter | [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Reference/Attribute/color-interpolation-filters) \[P\]; [Ana Tudor 2025](https://blog.master.dev/grainy-gradients/) \[P\] |
| Turbulence cost grows with octaves | stop at 3–4; filter region 0 0 1 1 | Tudor \[P\] |
| Filters soft on Retina | unverified: the 2015 bug cites a guess; test on the iPad | [WebKit 151737](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=151737) \[P\] |
| A costly layer redrawn every frame | draw once with createImageBitmap; redraw on a sun or colour change; give the SVG an explicit width and height | [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/createImageBitmap) \[P\] |
| iPad canvas size | 4096² up to iOS 17; 8192² (\~67 Mpx) from iOS 18 | [WebKit 271002](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=271002) \[P\]; [BrowserStack test, May 2025](https://lionpuro.com/posts/canvas-is-finally-usable-on-safari/) \[M\] |

How industry renders a sunburst: an anisotropic material in radial mode, roughness \~10× apart along and across ([KeyShot](https://support.keyshot.com/en/knowledge-base/knowledge/render-brushed-metals) \[P\]), after Ward's two-roughness highlight ([Ward 1992](https://radsite.lbl.gov/radiance/papers/sg92/paper.html) \[P\]). The highlight stretches across the grooves; the wedge recipe is the same idea, one value per wedge \[D\].

Metal from a picture: each facet takes its tone from a painted strip by which way it faces ([Lit Sphere, Graphics Interface 2001](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~bgooch/PDFs/LitSphere.pdf) \[P\]). Gooch 1998 already drew metal as \~20 light and dark stripes, the one nearest the light white ([PDF](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~bgooch/PDFs/gooch98.pdf) \[P\]); the closest ancestor of the wedge sunburst. The Lit Sphere's flaw, texture swimming in animation, doesn't apply to flat bevels.

### Adversarial pass

1. **Right problem or interesting one?** Interesting, twice over. v1 is the right problem and its open rows (#511, #506, #507, #510, #526, #536) are not about finishes or markets. The market sections change nothing in Now; they sharpen what the proof layer must get right when it comes, after v1.
2. **Real market or a nice story?** A nice story with a small market behind it. The counts are fair rosters, not customers: 150 brands at Windup include strap makers; Geneva's 71 includes brands at CHF 30,000. Each brand draws one to three dials a year and already owns KeyShot. Nobody pays a subscription for that frequency. The honest buyers are a bureau, a mod shop or a freelancer, a handful each \[D\].
3. **Least verified, and load-bearing.** The whole pain ranking rests on 25 incidents, 11 of them from unopened snippets; the richest first-person material (Kickstarter and BackerKit updates) sits behind the proxy. The sunburst bow-tie is still a derivation and a torch settles it. Every toner number is copper PCB, not brass. No per-unit dial price at 100, 300 or 500 was found anywhere public.
4. **The detail trap.** Two thirds of this document is how a finish looks under a sun and how a brand got a stone dial made. The watch Okay will build is etched brass with pad-printed ink, and the findings that bear on it stay few and blunt: toner-transfer lines of 0.2–0.4 mm, 0.5–1× the depth lost sideways per edge, numerals unmeasured. That needs a test plate, not a renderer and not a market study.
5. **What would break the cheap Next steps.** The Intel Mac's blur cost (#506) is unknown; the night is cheap only without bloom and grain. Exact-colour inks merge a lume white with a printed white, so lume can't land until the first question is answered. A Pantone on the spec sheet is meaningless until the tool knows which material the colour sits on.
6. **What got better.** Lume has an absolute end point; #545's keys make every "reserve it now" argument moot; the code holds a stroke-width estimate and an offset, so the production check is smaller than it looked; and the market reading says the proof layer is the only part of Dial Tool anyone outside this project would want, which is a useful narrowing.
