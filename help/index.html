Every pane and control in Dial Tool, and what it does.

Dial Tool, in short

A dial is a stack: the date disc, the plate with its print, the hands and the cap. The print is the rings drawn on the plate, and the ring list is that print, with the top ring first. You choose a ring, then edit it. Everything is true size, in millimetres. Your dial lives in this browser, and a settings file or a link keeps it for good.

The red box is where this sits on the screen. Press for the larger view.

Four ideas

A dial is a stack
Date disc, plate, print, hands, cap. The print is rings drawn on the plate.
The list is the print
The top ring wins where rings meet. A ring can give way to the rings above it.
Choose, then edit
Choose a row in the ring list, or tap a mark on the preview. The panes below edit that ring.
True size
Everything is in millimetres. Your dial is kept in this browser only: save a settings file to keep it, or share it as a link.

⑧Bottom bar

Rings

Rings are the print, built ring by ring from five kinds: ticks, markers, numerals, bands and text. The list stacks them with the top ring in front. When you choose one, the panes below show only what its kind uses: its font, size and radius, its shapes, where it stands round the dial, and whether it steps aside for the rings above. You can also edit a single numeral or mark on its own.

The red box is where this sits on the screen. Press for the larger view.

Windows

A dial can have up to four windows cut into the plate, one for each card: 12, 3, 6 and 9. A window can show the date, the day, am · pm or the moon, and each kind keeps its own settings. You set where it sits, the shape of its hole, its colours and frame, whether it follows the date, and the font it writes in.

The red box is where this sits on the screen. Press for the larger view.

Keys and touch

It is the same tool under a mouse, a finger and a keyboard. Keys work when you aren't typing in a field, and not with ⌘, Ctrl or ⌥ held; on Windows, Ctrl stands for ⌘. You can type a number as a sum or with a unit. Below are the places where mouse and finger differ.

Space
Play or pause.

The dial alone. It also ends Compare.

Undo and redo; ⌘Y redoes too. Not while a dialog is open.
↑ ↓ in a number
One step; with ⇧, ten. Enter or Esc takes the value; ⌘Z takes it back.
Esc
Closes an open menu or pop-up, the Guides list and the Shadows pane. Ends Compare.

Sums
+0.5 adds, x2 multiplies, /2 divides.
Units
A length takes a unit: 12pt, 0.5in, 3cm, 20lig, 40thou. A comma works as a point.

Controls

A slider
Mouse: drag it. Hold it and move away from the track, up or down, to go finer, down to a twentieth. Finger: a touch opens the controller; keep sliding and the value follows.
A row's name
Mouse: a press opens the controller; a sideways drag changes the value, ⇧ ten times finer. Finger: opens the controller.
The controller
The value over the knob, the slider, − and + with the name between. Esc, Enter or a press elsewhere closes it; it fades 1.2 s after you stop.
A number box
Type, or step with − and +. With a keyboard, ↑ ↓.

The dial

Move
Mouse: drag, or right-drag. Finger: two fingers. One finger never moves it, so a tap stays a tap.

Mouse: scroll, or pinch on a trackpad, about the pointer. Finger: pinch. On both, Zoom out, Zoom in and Fit on the bar; out to a quarter of Fit.

Words

These are the trade's words and the tool's own, as this page uses them. Each control's name is explained where the control is; here are the words that name parts of a watch, a print or a font.

A to Z

Anchor
The circle of a numerals or text ring that its radius names: Outer, Middle or Inner. It holds still when the size changes.
Band
A solid ring of colour.
Beat
How the seconds hand moves: sweep, quartz, 1/s, 6 or 8 beats a second.
Bevel
A hand's two long halves, one lit and one shaded. Preview only.
Big date
Two holes, tens and units, each on its own disc.
Cap
The disc over the pivot, part of the seconds hand.
Clearance
The gap a knock-out leaves round the marks above, or the print-free room round a window.
Complication
What a window shows: date, day, am · pm, moon, big date.
Counterweight
A disc on a hand's tail.
Disc
What turns under the plate and shows through a window.
Disc hand
A disc on the pivot carrying one marker, cut as a hole or painted.
First dial
The dial the tool opens with, and what Reset brings back.
Hollow wall
Turns a mark or a hand into an outline of that thickness.
Hub
The round boss at a hand's pivot.
Ink
In the Show Case, one exact colour printed on the dial, standing its height proud of the plate.
Kind
What a ring repeats: ticks, markers, numerals, band or text.
Knock out
A ring cut clear round the marks above it.
Live text · Outlines
Text kept as text in an SVG, needing the font where it opens; or drawn as shapes, needing none.
Marker
An hour mark; an index.
Mask
Black on white, for etching or film: every ring solid, no plate, no hands, the windows as outlines.
Mirrored
Flipped, for toner transfer.
Nudge
One position's own correction, from Edit one numeral or Edit one mark.
OpenType feature
A font's switch, such as tnum, onum, ss01 or zero, named as the font names it.
Pies
A band's sectors when its width reaches its radius.
Placement
How numerals stand: upright, radial, auto-flip, on path.
Plate
The dial disc under the print.
Railroad
A minute track of ticks between two thin rails: two thin bands.
Ring
One row in the ring list: a kind, repeated at its positions.
Spec sheet
The dial with its measures, for a supplier.
Tail
The part of a hand behind the pivot.
Taper
A wedge's inner width against its outer: 0 a triangle, 1 a bar.
Ticks
The minute track; a chapter ring.
Tracking
Even letter spacing, in thousandths of an em.
Variable axis
A variable font's sliding dimension, such as weight or width.
Weight %
Every width on the dial at once; not a font's weight.
Window
A hole in the plate showing a disc.
